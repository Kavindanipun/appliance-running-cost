How to calculate how much an appliance costs to run

You only need three things: the appliance's power, how long you use it, and your electricity price per kilowatt-hour (kWh).

The basic formula

Watts ÷ 1,000 = kilowattsKilowatts × hours = kWh usedkWh × electricity rate = running cost

For example, a 2,000W appliance is 2kW. If it draws that power for one hour, it uses 2kWh. Multiply that by your electricity unit rate to estimate the cost.

Where do I find the wattage?

Check the rating label on the back, bottom or power adapter. Manuals and manufacturer specifications may also list watts. If a device shows volts and amps instead, its real consumption can still vary, so a plug-in energy meter is often better for devices whose power changes during use.

Use your own electricity rate

Do not assume a generic national rate if you want a personal estimate. Look for the electricity unit rate on your bill. UK bills commonly express this in pence per kWh; US plans commonly show dollars or cents per kWh.

Why your actual bill can differ: thermostats cycle, compressors turn on and off, PCs change power with workload, dryers use different programmes, and tariffs can vary by time of day. This tool estimates energy cost from the inputs you provide; it does not include fixed standing/service charges.

How to lower appliance running costs

The biggest levers are usually power draw, run time and electricity price. Cutting unnecessary run time directly reduces the estimate. For flexible loads, a time-of-use tariff may make off-peak operation cheaper, depending on your plan. When comparing appliances, energy consumption over a typical cycle or year is usually more useful than purchase price alone.

Important limitations

This calculator is best for a device that draws roughly the entered wattage while operating. Appliances that cycle or vary power can use substantially less than “rated watts × total clock time.” If you know the appliance's measured kWh per cycle, day or year, use that consumption figure for a more accurate comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What does kWh mean?

A kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy. A 1,000W device drawing that power for one hour uses 1kWh.

Does a 2,000W heater always use 2kWh every hour?

Not necessarily. It would use 2kWh in an hour if it drew 2,000W continuously. A thermostat may switch the heating element on and off, reducing actual consumption.

Does this include standing charges or fixed utility fees?

No. It estimates the incremental energy cost of the appliance from your per-kWh rate. Fixed charges are excluded.

Can I use this in the UK?

Yes. Select United Kingdom and enter your electricity unit rate in pence per kWh. The calculator displays results in pounds.
